Vincent van Gogh painted this work during his stay at the asylum in Saint Rémy. He looked out from his window at dawn and saw the countryside under a broad sky filled with stars and the crescent moon. Although the village scene is partly imagined, the painting captures his profound engagement with nature and with his own inner state. He used the night sky as a stage for strong emotion, imagining the swirling heavens as a bridge between earth and something beyond. In his letters he described seeing the morning star “very big” above the wheat fields. The cypress tree in the foreground, rising into the sky, suggests yearning and connection.

Technically, van Gogh used oil on canvas with heavy impasto, meaning thick textured paint that creates visible ridges and gives the surface a physical presence. The brush strokes in the sky flow in broad swirling arcs, as though the sky itself is in motion. He selected intense blues such as ultramarine and cobalt for the sky, and brilliant yellows like indian yellow and zinc yellow for the stars and moon. The contrast between deep blue and bright yellow or orange intensifies the visual effect and renders the night scene with heightened emotion. He was not attempting a purely naturalistic image. He exaggerated form and color to express feeling.

In conclusion, The Starry Night stands as both a landscape and a vision. Through the choice of viewpoint, the exaggerated cypress, the dramatic sky, and the heavy texture of the paint, van Gogh transforms what could be a simple view into an emotional and spiritual tableau. The technical means, including impasto, directional brushwork, and a vivid palette, support this expressive intent. The painting invites the viewer to feel the turbulence of the sky, the silent village under the stars, and the longing of the artist reaching beyond the visible world.
