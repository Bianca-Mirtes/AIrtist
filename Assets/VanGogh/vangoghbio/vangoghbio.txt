Vincent van Gogh was a Dutch painter born in 1853 in the Netherlands. He worked for several years in art dealing before turning toward painting in his late twenties. His earliest works were dark studies of rural life, but after moving to Paris in 1886 he encountered Impressionist and Neo-Impressionist ideas that transformed his use of color and brushwork.

Van Gogh developed a highly personal style marked by vivid color, expressive strokes, and a focus on landscapes, portraits, and scenes of everyday life. Although he produced more than two thousand works, including over eight hundred oil paintings, he sold very little during his lifetime. His emotional struggles were significant, and he spent time in hospitals and asylums, continuing to paint even during these difficult periods.

He died in 1890 at the age of thirty seven. After his death his sister in law helped promote his work, and he eventually became one of the most influential and widely admired painters in Western art. His paintings are now recognized for their emotional intensity, technical innovation, and lasting cultural impact.
